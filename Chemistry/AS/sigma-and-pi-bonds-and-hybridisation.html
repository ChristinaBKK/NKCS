Spec 3.4.2(a) i–iii · orbital overlap

Two ways orbitals can overlap

A covalent bond forms when two atomic orbitals overlap and the shared pair sits between the nuclei. Pick an overlap below and watch it happen.

σ (sigma)π (pi)
OverlapEnd-on (head-on) overlap of s, p or hybrid orbitalsSideways overlap of two parallel p orbitals
Electron densityDirectly between the nuclei, along the bond axisAbove and below the bond axis, in two lobes
StrengthStrong — always the first bond between two atomsWeaker — only forms after a σ bond exists
RotationFree rotation about the bondPrevents rotation
Exam sentence — learn it word for word A σ bond forms by end-on overlap of orbitals along the internuclear axis; a π bond forms by sideways overlap of p orbitals, giving electron density above and below the axis.

Mark-scheme language

Say it the way Cambridge wants it

These questions are worth easy marks — if your wording hits the marking points. Click each card to flip it.

“Describe how a σ bond forms.”

Click to reveal the mark-scheme answer →

End-on (head-on) overlap of two orbitals along the internuclear axis, concentrating electron density directly between the nuclei.

“Describe how a π bond forms.”

Click to reveal the mark-scheme answer →

Sideways overlap of two parallel p orbitals, giving electron density in two lobes above and below the σ bond axis.

“Describe the bonding in N₂.”

Click to reveal the mark-scheme answer →

Each N is sp hybridised: one sp–sp σ bond, then two π bonds at right angles from sideways overlap of the 2py and 2pz orbitals; one lone pair per N.

“Why can't a C=C bond rotate?”

Click to reveal the mark-scheme answer →

Rotating would twist the parallel p orbitals out of alignment and break the π bond — the σ bond alone allows rotation, the π bond locks it.

Common slip: saying a double bond is “two σ bonds”, or that π electron density sits between the nuclei. Both cost the mark.

Topic 3.4.2 · complete

The whole topic in five lines

σ bond — end-on overlap along the internuclear axis; the strong first bond; allows rotation.
π bond — sideways overlap of parallel p orbitals; density above and below the axis; locks rotation.
Single = 1σ · double = 1σ + 1π · triple = 1σ + 2π (π bonds at right angles).
N₂: sp on each N → 1σ + 2π + a lone pair each. HCN: sp on C → σ to H, σ + 2π to N, lone pair on N.
sp = linear 180° · sp² = trigonal planar 120° · sp³ = tetrahedral 109.5°.
Hybridisation = count σ bonds + lone pairs around the atom; π bonds come from leftover p orbitals.
